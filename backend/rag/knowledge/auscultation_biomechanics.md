# PHONOCARDIOGRAM ACOUSTICS & AUSCULTATORY BIOMECHANICS

### Aortic Stenosis (AS) Acoustic Profile & Biomechanics
- **Murmur Morphology:** Harsh, raspy crescendo-decrescendo (diamond-shaped) mid-systolic ejection murmur.
- **Timing Relative to S1/S2:** Initiates shortly after S1 (following isovolumetric contraction and aortic valve opening). Intensity peaks in mid-to-late systole depending on obstruction severity, and terminates strictly prior to the aortic closure sound (A2). Late-peaking murmur signifies severe orifice narrowing.
- **Topographical Mapping & Transmission:** Maximal acoustic amplitude at the Right 2nd Intercostal Space (Parasternal Aortic Area). Strong anatomical acoustic radiation along high-velocity turbulent blood flow vectors superiorly and rightward toward the carotid arteries (right carotid > left carotid).
- **Postural Maneuvers & Acoustic Biomechanics:**
  - Upright / Sitting Leaning Forward: Substantial acoustic accentuation. Sitting leaning forward brings the Left Ventricular Outflow Tract (LVOT) and aortic root into direct geometric proximity to the anterior chest wall, minimizing soft-tissue acoustic attenuation.
  - Expiration: Accentuated during passive expiration as pulmonary venous return to the left heart increases, augmenting left ventricular stroke volume and transvalvular jet velocity.
- **Secondary Auscultatory Signs:** Single or paradoxically split S2 (delayed A2 closure occurring after P2), diminished or absent A2 due to heavy calcific leaflet rigidity, and presence of an S4 gallop indicating vigorous atrial contraction into a non-compliant, hypertrophied left ventricle.

### Aortic Regurgitation (AR) Acoustic Profile & Biomechanics
- **Murmur Morphology:** High-pitched, blowing early-diastolic decrescendo murmur.
- **Timing Relative to S1/S2:** Begins immediately at the aortic component of S2 (A2) with maximal instantaneous retrograde pressure gradient, tapering progressively throughout diastole as aortic and left ventricular diastolic pressures equilibrate.
- **Topographical Mapping & Transmission:** Heard best at the Left 3rd/4th Intercostal Space along the Left Sternal Border (Erb's point) for primary valvular disease, or Right 2nd ICS if secondary to aortic root dilatation. Murmur radiates toward the cardiac apex.
- **Postural Maneuvers & Acoustic Biomechanics:**
  - Upright / Sitting Leaning Forward with Full Expiration: Maximally accentuated. Full end-expiration lowers lung volume and cardiac acoustic impedance, optimizing detection of high-frequency retrograde diastolic regurgitant jets.
- **Secondary Auscultatory Signs:**
  - Austin Flint Murmur: Low-pitched mid-to-late diastolic rumbling at the cardiac apex caused by the regurgitant aortic jet impinging on the anterior leaflet of the mitral valve, causing functional mitral inflow narrowing without organic mitral stenosis.
  - Wide pulse pressure signs (water-hammer pulse, systolic ejection flow murmur across aortic valve due to hyperdynamic stroke volume).

### Mitral Regurgitation (MR) Acoustic Profile & Biomechanics
- **Murmur Morphology:** High-pitched, blowing holosystolic (pansystolic) plateau murmur with uniform acoustic intensity throughout systole.
- **Timing Relative to S1/S2:** Begins precisely at S1 (coinciding with mitral closure and ventricular pressure exceeding left atrial pressure) and persists throughout systole up to and through S2, maintaining regurgitant flow as long as LV pressure exceeds LA pressure.
- **Topographical Mapping & Transmission:** Maximal acoustic intensity at the Cardiac Apex (Left 5th Intercostal Space, Midclavicular Line). Strong acoustic radiation along the posterior leaflet jet direction laterally into the left axilla and left infrascapular region.
- **Postural Maneuvers & Acoustic Biomechanics:**
  - Left Lateral Decubitus Posture: Substantial acoustic amplification. Rolling the patient into the left lateral position displaces the cardiac apex into direct contact with the thoracic wall, maximizing signal-to-noise ratio.
  - Isometric Handgrip (Afterload augmentation): Murmur intensifies due to increased systemic vascular resistance augmenting retrograde trans-mitral pressure gradient.
- **Secondary Auscultatory Signs:** Diminished or buried S1, prominent S3 gallop indicating rapid ventricular filling into a volume-overloaded, dilated left ventricle.

### Mitral Stenosis (MS) Acoustic Profile & Biomechanics
- **Murmur Morphology:** Low-pitched, rumbling mid-diastolic murmur with presystolic accentuation in patients in normal sinus rhythm.
- **Timing Relative to S1/S2:** Initiates with an Opening Snap (OS) in early diastole shortly after S2 (coinciding with the sudden tensing of the stenotic mitral leaflets upon valve opening). Followed by the mid-diastolic inflow rumble, ending with presystolic accentuation due to atrial contraction.
- **Topographical Mapping & Transmission:** Highly localized to the Cardiac Apex (Left 5th ICS Midclavicular Line) over an area as small as a 50-cent coin. Minimal or no acoustic radiation.
- **Postural Maneuvers & Acoustic Biomechanics:**
  - Left Lateral Decubitus with Bell Application: Bell of stethoscope with light pressure is essential due to the low-frequency acoustic energy (<100 Hz).
  - Post-Exercise / Dynamic Leg Raise: Murmur markedly accentuates as tachycardia and elevated cardiac output shorten diastole and raise the trans-mitral pressure gradient.
- **Secondary Auscultatory Signs:** Loud, snapping S1 (forceful closure of pliable leaflets), narrow A2-OS interval (shorter interval signifies higher left atrial pressure and more severe stenosis), loud P2 if secondary pulmonary hypertension is present.

### Physiological / Normal Cardiac Auscultation
- **Normal Heart Sounds:**
  - First Heart Sound (S1): Crisp, synchronous closure of mitral and tricuspid valves, loudest at the apex and lower left sternal border.
  - Second Heart Sound (S2): Dual component (A2 and P2). Physiological splitting observed during inspiration (A2 precedes P2 by 30-60 ms due to increased venous return delaying pulmonic closure and reduced pulmonary venous return hastening aortic closure). Synchronous single sound during expiration.
- **Absence of Pathology:** No systolic ejection murmurs, no holosystolic murmurs, no early diastolic decrescendo murmurs, and complete absence of diastolic rumbles, opening snaps, or pathological S3/S4 gallops.
