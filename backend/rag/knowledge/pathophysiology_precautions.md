# PATHOPHYSIOLOGY, DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES & CLINICAL MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### Aortic Stenosis (AS) Pathophysiology, Differentials & Management
- **Pathophysiology & Remodeling:**
  - Chronic fixed obstruction generates high systolic pressure overload on the left ventricle.
  - Triggers compensatory concentric Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH) via parallel addition of sarcomeres, preserving wall stress (Laplace's Law) at the expense of diastolic non-compliance, elevated LV filling pressures, and reduced subendocardial coronary perfusion reserve.
- **Differential Considerations:**
  - Aortic Sclerosis: Differentiated by normal peak aortic velocity (Vmax < 2.0 m/s) and absence of transvalvular gradient or LVH.
  - Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM): Dynamic subaortic outflow obstruction; murmur intensifies with Valsalva maneuver or standing (which decrease LV preload), whereas fixed valvular AS murmur diminishes.
  - Subvalvular or Supravalvular Stenosis: Distinct echocardiographic anatomical localization.
- **Critical Precautions & Contraindications:**
  - Avoid strenuous exertion or competitive athletics pending definitive echocardiographic grading.
  - Caution with Vasodilators: Avoid abrupt or high-dose afterload-reducing vasodilators (e.g., ACE inhibitors, Angiotensin Receptor Blockers, hydralazine, and nitrates) in severe fixed LVOT obstruction, as sudden systemic vasodilation cannot be compensated by an increase in cardiac output across the stenotic valve, risking profound hypotension, syncope, and coronary hypoperfusion.
- **Intervention Triage:**
  - Class I Indication for Intervention: Symptomatic severe AS (Stage D) or asymptomatic severe AS with LVEF < 50% (Stage C2).
  - Modality: Heart Valve Team assessment based on age, STS/EuroSCORE II surgical risk, and anatomical feasibility:
    - Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR): Preferred in patients aged >= 80 years or high/prohibitive surgical risk; alternative in intermediate risk.
    - Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement (SAVR): Preferred in patients aged < 65 years or with anatomy unsuitable for TAVR, bicuspid valves, or concomitant aortopathy.

### Aortic Regurgitation (AR) Pathophysiology, Differentials & Management
- **Pathophysiology & Remodeling:**
  - Diastolic backflow into the left ventricle imposes combined volume and pressure overload.
  - Compensatory eccentric hypertrophy (chamber dilation with wall thickening) maintains forward stroke volume. High systolic pressure paired with low diastolic pressure produces classic wide pulse pressure.
- **Differential Considerations:**
  - Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA): Continuous machinery murmur, unlike isolated diastolic AR murmur.
  - Mitral Stenosis: Distinguished from the Austin Flint murmur of severe AR (Austin Flint lacks opening snap and loud S1).
  - Sinus of Valsalva Aneurysm Rupture: Acute continuous murmur.
- **Critical Precautions & Contraindications:**
  - Avoid Beta-Blockers in Acute or Severe Chronic AR: Beta-blockers prolong diastolic filling time, allowing a longer duration for retrograde diastolic regurgitation and worsening LV volume overload and pulmonary congestion.
- **Intervention Triage:**
  - Class I Indication: Symptomatic severe AR (Stage D), or asymptomatic severe AR with LV systolic dysfunction (LVEF <= 55%, Stage C2), or severe LV dilation (LVESD > 50 mm).

### Mitral Regurgitation (MR) Pathophysiology, Differentials & Management
- **Pathophysiology & Remodeling:**
  - Systolic backflow into the low-pressure left atrium causes pure volume overload.
  - Triggers left atrial enlargement and eccentric LV hypertrophy. Chronic volume overload leads to myocardial stretch, atrial fibrillation, and late irreversible LV systolic failure.
- **Differential Considerations:**
  - Tricuspid Regurgitation (TR): TR murmur intensifies with inspiration (Carvallo's sign); MR murmur does not change or slightly softens.
  - Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD): Harsh holosystolic murmur with thrill, maximal at left lower sternal border.
  - Aortic Stenosis: AS murmur is ejection-type with pause before S2; MR murmur is holosystolic and encompasses S2.
- **Critical Precautions & Management:**
  - Guideline-directed medical therapy for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (GDMT) if secondary MR.
  - Atrial Fibrillation Management: Rhythm/rate control and systemic thromboembolism risk stratification (CHA2DS2-VASc score) with oral anticoagulation.
- **Intervention Triage:**
  - Primary MR: Mitral valve repair is preferred over replacement when performed in experienced centers. Class I in symptomatic severe MR (Stage D) or asymptomatic severe MR with LVEF <= 60% or LVESD >= 40 mm (Stage C2).
  - Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair (TEER): Indicated for high surgical risk primary MR or guideline-eligible secondary MR.

### Mitral Stenosis (MS) Pathophysiology, Differentials & Management
- **Pathophysiology & Remodeling:**
  - Mechanical obstruction to LV diastolic inflow raises left atrial pressure and LA volume.
  - Causes passive and reactive pulmonary venous hypertension, pulmonary arterial hypertension, right ventricular pressure overload, and secondary tricuspid regurgitation.
  - Left ventricular cavity is typically normal in size or underfilled.
- **Differential Considerations:**
  - Left Atrial Myxoma: "Tumor plop" can mimic opening snap; positional variability of murmur.
  - Austin Flint Murmur of severe AR: Absent opening snap; responds to afterload reduction.
  - Tricuspid Stenosis: Extremely rare, intensifies with inspiration.
- **Critical Precautions & Management:**
  - Heart Rate Control: Tachycardia dramatically shortens diastolic filling time, causing sudden steep rises in LA pressure and flash pulmonary edema. Beta-blockers or non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers are indicated to prolong diastole.
  - Systemic Anticoagulation: Class I indication for Vitamin K Antagonist (Warfarin) in MS with atrial fibrillation or prior thromboembolic event, regardless of CHA2DS2-VASc score (DOACs are generally not approved for moderate-to-severe rheumatic MS).
- **Intervention Triage:**
  - Percutaneous Mitral Balloon Commissurotomy (PMBC): First-line therapy for symptomatic severe MS (MVA <= 1.5 cm²) with favorable valve anatomy (Wilkins echocardiographic score <= 8) and absence of left atrial thrombus or moderate-to-severe MR.
  - Surgical Mitral Valve Replacement: Indicated when valve anatomy is heavily calcified/subvalvular fusion is severe (Wilkins score > 8) or PMBC is contraindicated.

### Physiological / Normal Cardiac Auscultation Pathophysiology, Differentials & Management
- **Pathophysiology & Remodeling:**
  - Normal intracardiac laminar flow across all four anatomical cardiac valves. Normal left and right ventricular chamber volumes and filling pressures.
- **Differential Considerations:**
  - Innocent Flow Murmurs: Soft, vibratory systolic noise heard in young, active, or pregnant patients without valvular or structural pathology.
  - Aortic Sclerosis: Mild focal leaflet thickening without transvalvular gradient acceleration (< 2.0 m/s).
- **Critical Precautions & Management:**
  - Maintain routine active lifestyle and routine preventive health practices.
- **Intervention Triage:**
  - No medical therapy, surgical intervention, or catheter-based valve intervention is indicated.
